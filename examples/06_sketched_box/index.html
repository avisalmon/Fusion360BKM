06 · Sketched box: from a paper note to an object

The owner drew a box on paper, photographed it with a phone and dropped the photo into the chat with one line: "The next example is read from paper sketch and design." In the same chat session the tray existed in Fusion, checked by numbers and by eye, with its archive exported. Nothing was typed into Fusion by a human.

1. The paper note: what was read

The photo was taken with the paper turned 90 degrees, so the first act was to rotate it upright (media/brief.jpg). Then, following the sketch-to-design skill, read it in order: the view and which way is up; every number and the edge it belongs to; the shapes; and what is not there.

Not on it: whether the top is open (the inner lines suggested it), where the four holes are (the dashed circles sit low on the walls, where a floor hole shows through a wall in an isometric view: hidden lines) and how far from the edges, and whether the corners are rounded. Each gap became one question; none was filled silently.

3. The script, step by step

d, r = new_design("SketchedBox")

add_param("length", "100 mm", "mm", "outer length (the 100 on the sketch)")
add_param("width", "80 mm", "mm", "outer width (the 80)")
add_param("height", "27 mm", "mm", "outer height (the 27)")
add_param("wall", "2 mm", "mm", "wall and floor thickness ('2mm thick')")
add_param("hole_d", "6 mm", "mm", "hole diameter ('4 holes 6 mm')")
add_param("hole_inset", "10 mm", "mm", "hole centre to outer edge (GUESS: not on the sketch)")

sk = sketch_on("xy", name="outline")            # 1. the footprint, centred on the origin
prect(sk, "length", "width")                     #    a fully constrained, parametric rectangle
tray = extrude(sk, "height", "new").bodies.item(0)   # 2. up by `height`
tray.name = "Tray"
shell(tray, top_face(tray), "wall")             # 3. open the top, leave `wall` everywhere

hx = eval_mm("length/2 - hole_inset")            # 4. hole centres computed from the parameters
hy = eval_mm("width/2 - hole_inset")
sk2 = sketch_on("xy", name="floor holes")        #    sketched on the underside (z = 0) ...
for sx in (-1, 1):
    for sy in (-1, 1):
        circle(sk2, sx * hx, sy * hy, eval_mm("hole_d"))
extrude(sk2, "wall", "cut", toward=(0, 0, 1))    # 5. ... and cut upward through the floor

fit()
result = {"doc": active_doc_name(), "bodies": body_info(), "params": params()}

Five features, in the order a person would do them by hand. The rectangle, the extrude and the shell stay linked to the parameters: change height in Modify › Change Parameters and the tray grows. The hole positions are numbers computed from the parameters when the script runs, because circle() is a numeric helper (see below). The script ends with result, so a run prints the evidence itself.

What it taught this blueprint

  • Reading a photo of a sketch works when you read it in order: view and up-direction, every number with its edge, shapes, then the gaps; each gap becomes one question with a recommended answer. The owner's three answers took under a minute. Recorded in the sketch-to-design skill, whose status no longer says "not practised yet".
  • Dashed circles on a hand drawing are hidden lines. The three dashed holes sat low on the walls, exactly where a floor hole shows through a wall in an isometric view, and Fusion's own isometric reproduced the pattern. Trust the drafting convention before guessing "holes in the walls".
  • Numeric versus parametric helpers. circle(), hole_at() and pattern_rect() take numbers; prect(), extrude() and shell() take expressions. A fully parametric design needs a parametric circle (backlog, Epic 2); until then eval_mm("length/2 - hole_inset") makes the numbers follow the parameters at build time.
  • Cut holes from the underside. A sketch on the XY plane at z = 0 plus extrude(..., "wall", "cut", toward=(0, 0, 1)) avoids sketching on a face, whose sketch axes may not be the model's; the face count proves the cut went through.
  • Gotcha 16: in a script run through the bridge, the helpers' private names are not there (_app raised NameError); use app, design(), root() as the skills show.
  • Fusion's own camera makes the eye check honest: ViewOrientations.IsoTopRightViewOrientation, Top and Bottom (the fusion-files recipe) gave the drawing's view and two more without a human touching the mouse.
